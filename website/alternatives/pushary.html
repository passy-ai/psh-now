vs Pushary

An open source alternative to Pushary

Pushary solves the same problem: an agent stops, a person answers, and the answer goes back to the agent. It pushes to iOS, Android and the Mac notch, and it applies per-tool policy in hooks. psh.now holds one HTTP call open, is MIT licensed, and is priced per request; the differences are below.

What Pushary is, in its own terms

The points below are what Pushary publishes on pushary.com and in its llms.txt. If any of it changes, their site is the source of truth, not this page.

A hosted service, at a per-seat price

A plan is required after the trial: $59 a month for five people, then $12 per person.

Push to the lock screen

A confirm question can be answered from the iOS or Android lock screen. Multiple-choice and free-text questions open the app. Also the Mac notch, Slack and n8n.

Per-tool policy, enforced in hooks

Each tool rule can auto-approve, deny, escalate or time out, applied where the agent supports hooks or the application gates the action itself.

SDKs and framework adapters

An MCP server, TypeScript and Python SDKs under MIT, plus adapters for the Vercel AI SDK, LangGraph, LangChain, CrewAI, Mastra, the OpenAI Agents SDK and others.

Where the two differ

Ten rows that change a decision. Where Pushary is the better answer, the row says so.

Pick Pushary when

A lock-screen answer

Pushary sends to iOS and Android, and a confirm question is answered on the lock screen. psh.now sends web push to the installed dashboard, and the answer happens in the dashboard.

You want policy in the hook

A rule that blocks a tool call before it runs is a stronger guarantee than an agent that is trusted to honour the reply. Pushary sits in that hook; psh.now returns a decision and the agent acts on it.

You need more than yes or no

The agent needs one route chosen out of three, say. Pushary offers multiple choice; psh.now takes one approve or deny, or one typed answer, and rejects anything shaped like a menu.

Slack is where the team lives

Delivering into an existing room, and taking the answer back from there, is a solved problem for them.

Pick psh.now when

The approval path has to be inspectable

MIT on the server and on the skill. You can read the code that decides whether your agent proceeds, and change the part you disagree with.

The question cannot leave your network

Self-hosted: a Bun service and Postgres, started with docker compose up. Requests, keys and history stay in your own database.

You do not want a per-seat meter

Pricing counts requests rather than seats. An approval costs two requests, a request that expires or is cancelled costs nothing, and 10,000 requests cost 50 EUR prepaid.

The whole integration is one POST

One POST with a title and a message, and the decision comes back in the open call. Nothing to install on the agent side; a CLI, a hook and an MCP endpoint are there if you want them.

What psh.now does not do

The gaps, stated plainly.

  • No blocking of the tool call. psh.now returns a decision; the agent still has to honour it. A per-tool rule that stops the call before it runs sits in the hook, and that is Pushary's design.
  • No multiple choice. One approve, one deny, or one typed answer. A message shaped like a menu is rejected, because one tap cannot answer it.
  • No Slack and no n8n. The surfaces are the dashboard, the notch app on macOS, and the agent's own terminal.
  • A request expires. 90 seconds by default, 15 minutes at most. When the window closes the call returns expired, and that is not an approval.

Where those four matter more than licence, self-hosting and price, Pushary is the better product. That is the reading of the table above, not a sales line.
